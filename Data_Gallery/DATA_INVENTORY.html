4. If they challenge you — short answers

"Why satellite data and not measured data?"

"Nigeria's ground-station network is sparse and gappy — I state this in my limitations. The accepted method for data-scarce basins is to use validated satellite and reanalysis data and bias-check it against local records, which I did (I compared CHIRPS rain against NIMET)."

"Did you really download and build this yourself?"

"Yes — the original files are still on my machine, and every command, source link and setting is in my methodology log. I can open any of them right now."

"You calibrated against GEOGLOWS — that's a model, not a real gauge."

"Correct, and I'm open about it. Real gauge data from NIHSA is slow and incomplete, so I used GEOGLOWS modelled discharge as the benchmark — a recognised data-scarce approach — and reported performance at the monthly step. It's documented as a limitation, not hidden."

Part B — The AHP flood-susceptibility map: its 8 factors (statewide Lagos)

How the map works (say this): the map scores every 30 m cell in Lagos for flood-proneness. Each factor below is stretched to a 0–1 scale, multiplied by its AHP weight (the 8 weights add up to 1.00 and were set using an Analytic Hierarchy Process pairwise comparison), then all are added into one Flood Hazard Index (0–1). That index is finally cut into 5 zones: Very Low → Very High. Watch the direction: for some factors a low value means high hazard (elevation, slope, distance to rivers); for others a high value does (TWI, flow accumulation, runoff, rainfall).

The 8 weighted factors

Elevation AHP weight 0.20

What it is: Height of the land above sea level. Lagos is very low — mostly 0–47 m.

Why we need it: The biggest driver. Water collects in the lowest places, so LOW ground = HIGH hazard. Lagos is a low, coastal city, which is why elevation carries the largest weight.

How we got it: Read from a 30 m satellite elevation model (SRTM / Copernicus-class DEM). [open source]

TWI — Topographic Wetness Index AHP weight 0.18

What it is: An index of how much water tends to gather at each spot (combines how much land drains to it and how flat it is).

Why we need it: HIGH TWI = water pools there = HIGH hazard. It finds the wet, flat hollows where floodwater sits.

How we got it: Calculated from the DEM in GIS: ln(upslope area ÷ slope).

Distance to rivers AHP weight 0.15

What it is: How far each point is from the nearest river or drainage channel (metres).

Why we need it: Rivers overflow first, so CLOSE to a river = HIGH hazard.

How we got it: Rivers were traced from the DEM's flow network, then a distance layer was built in QGIS.
